## AI YOU Town: Make Friends and Money with Your Digital Twin

- **Authors / venue / year:** Yan Lin, Yuyang Dai, Jiahui Geng, Yuxia Wang (INSAIT / Newcastle / Linköping) — 2026 (arXiv, 2026-07-12).
- **arXiv / DOI:** 2607.10539v1 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10539
- **Source:** discovered
- **Overall relevance to longmem-npc:** High — directly on our persona/character-consistency lane, with ablation evidence for a concrete, cheap mechanism (periodic identity-anchor refresh) that maps onto our sequenced-but-undesigned Reflection pipeline, plus a calibrated-uncertainty idea we have no analogue of.
- **Core contribution (2-3 sentences):** AI YOU maintains a 22-dimensional personality profile inferred from conversation with Bayesian posterior updating and conformal prediction sets for calibrated uncertainty, embodied via a "persona memory anchor" that a dedicated writer refreshes every k=10 turns (folding in the current anchor + recent dialogue + trait constraints) rather than re-prompting from a static system prompt. Across 100-turn persona-preservation evaluations (including an adversarial multi-agent Werewolf setting), periodic anchor refresh beats static full-history prompting on fidelity and reduces trait drift for most backbones.

### Mechanisms relevant to us
- **Persona memory anchor with periodic refresh** (§3.3): "instead of using a static persona prompt, AI YOU maintains a structured persona memory anchor A_t that is continuously updated throughout interaction... Every k turns (k=10 by default), a dedicated memory writer updates the anchor using the current anchor state, recent dialogue history, and trait constraints."
- **Three-layer cognitive memory** (§3.4): working (20-turn FIFO) → episodic (compressed every 10 turns, with "periodic consistency checks compare episodic summaries against archived dialogue and repair inconsistent summaries when necessary") → semantic (reflects over episodic every 50 turns into "candidate long-term beliefs... incorporated only when supported by sufficient evidence").
- **Bayesian posterior + conformal calibration for trait/attribute uncertainty** (§3.1): Gaussian conjugate updates per numeric trait with confidence-derived observation variance, plus conformal prediction sets (coverage 0.921-0.976) so every inferred attribute carries a calibrated confidence, not just a point value.
- **Persona-preservation ablation** (Table 7): refresh vs. static full-history prompting over 100-turn dialogues — refresh improves all fidelity dimensions (in-character +0.84, style +0.91, knowledge +0.85, overall +0.87, on a 1-5 LLM-judge scale) and reduces Big-Five trait drift under adversarial pressure (Werewolf) for 3 of 4 backbones, most strongly for GPT-4o-mini (0.008 vs. 0.036 mean absolute deviation).

### NOT-YET-BUILT candidates (a capability we simply don't have)
- **Capability:** periodic, evidence-driven identity-document refresh, as a concrete mechanism for our sequenced-later Reflection pipeline's "revises seed identity" step.
- **What the paper does:** a fixed-cadence (every k turns) rewrite of a structured identity anchor, explicitly conditioned on the *prior* anchor plus recent dialogue plus trait constraints — not a from-scratch re-derivation, and not a static prompt held for the whole session (§3.3). Ablation shows this concretely beats the "just keep the full history in context" alternative on both fidelity and drift resistance (Table 7, §4.7).
- **Why worth adopting for an NPC memory service:** our `identity_documents` table + `identity_version` plumbing already exist (built with reconstruction, 2026-07-17), but the identity doc itself is seed-prose-only and static until reflection lands — reflection currently has no specified cadence or update mechanism, only "revises seed identity... prunes the identity-components table," undesigned. AI YOU Town's anchor-refresh gives a concretely evidenced, lightweight update rule (current-anchor + recent-window + constraints → new anchor) that's a plausible starting shape for reflection's first cut, and its ablation is direct evidence that *not* refreshing (i.e., our current seed-prose-only state) costs measurable persona fidelity and drift resistance over long play.
- **Adoption cost/risk in our stack:** low-to-medium as a design reference (reflection is already sequenced, not yet specced — this doesn't jump the queue, it informs the spec when that session happens); the mechanism itself doesn't need a new migration since `identity_documents`/`identity_version` already exist for exactly this purpose.
- **Docs it would touch:** the eventual reflection spec (sequenced-later, mechanism undesigned) — architecture §7/§4.3 identity plumbing notes.
- **Confidence:** High (mechanism clearly described, ablation-quantified) for informing reflection's design; the k=10-turn cadence itself is a hyperparameter choice from their benchmark, not a value to import literally.

- **Capability:** calibrated uncertainty (not just a point score) for identity/trait attributes.
- **What the paper does:** Bayesian conjugate updating gives a posterior mean *and* variance per trait; conformal prediction wraps this into a coverage-guaranteed prediction set (§3.1, §4.2).
- **Why worth adopting for an NPC memory service:** every scalar we track today (`importance_raw`, `reputation`, `rigidity`, `diagnosticity_goal`) is a point value with no uncertainty representation — we can't currently distinguish "the NPC is confidently disagreeable" from "we've seen one data point suggesting disagreeable." This matters most wherever reflection eventually infers durable traits from sparse evidence, exactly AI YOU's use case.
- **Adoption cost/risk in our stack:** medium — would mean widening whatever reflection/identity-inference schema eventually lands to carry a variance/confidence column alongside each trait, and deciding whether conformal calibration is worth the complexity for a game-NPC context (their eval is human-facing digital twins with adversarial-actor concerns; our NPCs may not need coverage guarantees, just a usable confidence signal).
- **Docs it would touch:** the eventual reflection spec.
- **Confidence:** Medium — genuinely new to our design, but its value for a game NPC (vs. a digital-twin-for-real-humans product) is less obviously load-bearing; flagged for Jack's judgment rather than a strong recommendation.

### THESIS-TENSION flags (conflicts with an invariant — surface, don't force)
- **Invariant challenged:** #1, bi-temporal non-destructive storage.
- **What the paper does that conflicts:** "periodic consistency checks compare episodic summaries against archived dialogue and repair inconsistent summaries when necessary" (§3.4) — this reads as in-place correction of a stored summary, not a versioned supersede. The persona anchor itself is also described as being "continuously updated," not chained.
- **Honest read:** apples-to-oranges in part — AI YOU Town's memory layers are explicitly *working state* for generation (closer in spirit to our `agents.reputation`/`memories.pinned` deliberate in-place scalars than to our `memory_details`/`memory_fact_versions` chains), and the paper never claims an auditable ground-truth record underneath. It's not a direct critique of our design so much as a reminder that "periodic refresh" as a mechanism is orthogonal to whether the refresh target is versioned or mutated — if we adopt the refresh-cadence idea for our identity document, the refresh itself should still write a new `identity_documents` row (bumping `identity_version`) rather than mutate the existing one, consistent with what we already do.

### Quotable lines / citations for positioning (optional)
- "Prompt-assigned personas often drift during extended conversations: models contradict earlier statements, drift in tone, and gradually abandon role-appropriate behavior... Simply assigning a persona at the beginning is insufficient to prevent this drift." (§2.1) — directly validates the motivating premise behind our reconstruction/decay-band mechanism and is a clean, citable line for the README's positioning section.
- "Larger models may exhibit greater identity instability than smaller ones." (§2.1, citing Choi et al. 2024) — a useful caution against assuming a bigger dialogue model alone solves persona consistency.

### Verdict
P2 worth-piloting: no code should change now (reflection is correctly sequenced-later and undesigned), but this paper should be a first reference when reflection is specced — its ablation is real evidence that periodic, evidence-conditioned identity refresh beats both static prompting and full-history stuffing, which is exactly the choice our reflection spec will eventually have to make.
