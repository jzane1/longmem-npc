# When to Forget: A Memory Governance Primitive (Memory Worth)

- **Authors / venue / year:** Baris Simsek — April 2026, arXiv preprint.
- **arXiv / DOI:** arXiv:2604.12007v1 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12007
- **Source:** discovered
- **Overall relevance to longmem-npc:** Medium — proposes a lightweight, theoretically-grounded *outcome-linked* staleness/quality signal that is architecturally distinct from anything we have (our decay is pure elapsed-time math; our importance is a write-time LLM score); it's a plausible future input to reflection/pruning, but requires an "outcome" signal our current turn loop doesn't cleanly produce.
- **Core contribution (2-3 sentences):** Memory Worth (MW) is a two-counter per-memory statistic — weighted counts of co-occurrence with successful vs. failed task outcomes — that provably converges almost-surely to the true conditional success probability `p+(m) = Pr[success | m retrieved]` under stated assumptions (stationarity, minimum exploration, conditional independence of retrieval from outcome). The paper is unusually rigorous about scope: it proves convergence, then runs four "assumption-violation" experiments showing the signal breaks (sometimes in the *wrong direction*, e.g. −0.33 correlation) under realistic confounds like task-difficulty correlation and co-retrieval ("hitchhiker") effects, and only partially recovers with conditioning. A fifth experiment with real text and neural embedding retrieval (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) shows a designed-to-go-stale memory's MW score correctly crossing a low-value threshold over 3,000 episodes while a specialist memory's stays high.

### Mechanisms relevant to us
- **Memory Worth definition** (§3.2, Eq. 1): `MW_T(m) = hits+_T(m) / (hits+_T(m) + hits-_T(m))`, weighted retrieval counts of co-occurrence with success (`y=+1`) vs. failure (`y=-1`); uninformative prior (0.5) when unretrieved. Requires only two scalars per memory, updated online from existing retrieval/outcome logs — no architectural change, no LLM judgment.
- **Two-count (not single-ratio) representation with an evidence-aware taxonomy** (§3.4): high-value / uncertain / mixed-outcome / low-value, distinguished by both the success ratio *and* total evidence volume `V_m` — critically, this separates "insufficient evidence" from "genuinely mixed outcomes," which a single scalar cannot.
- **Convergence theorem with explicit, honestly-scoped assumptions** (§4, A1–A6) and a companion set of experiments quantifying exactly how much the signal degrades when each assumption is violated in a realistic way (task-difficulty confound: -0.33; co-retrieval/"hitchhiker" confound: near-total conflation until ≥30% of retrievals break the pairing).
- **The explicit distinction from write-time importance:** "Park et al. (2023) introduce a three-component retrieval score combining recency, LLM-assigned importance, and embedding relevance. The importance score is the closest precursor to Memory Worth, but it is static — assigned once at write time and never updated by outcomes" (§2) — this is the same critique that would apply to our `importance_raw` (scored once at write time per our importance-vs-relevance independent-axes invariant).

### STRICTLY-BETTER candidates (beats a mechanism we already have)
*(none — our decay math (`tau_effective`) and importance scoring are deliberately write-time/elapsed-time mechanisms serving a different purpose (recency and diagnosticity), not outcome-linked quality tracking; MW is a different axis entirely, not a replacement.)*

### NOT-YET-BUILT candidates (a capability we simply don't have)
- **Capability:** An outcome-linked, online memory-quality signal for staleness/deprecation decisions, distinct from both decay (elapsed time) and importance (write-time LLM score) — no equivalent exists in our baseline; closest adjacent gaps are "Reflection sampling strategy beyond importance×recency — undesigned" and the general lack of a governance signal for pruning `identity_components` or deprioritizing decayed memories beyond pure time.
- **What the paper does:** Tracks, per memory, weighted counts of retrieval co-occurring with a later positive vs. negative outcome, proven to converge to the true conditional success probability, and demonstrated to correctly identify an intentionally-staled memory (dropping to MW=0.17 by episode 3000) against a genuinely useful "specialist" memory (stable at 0.77) under realistic embedding retrieval — *evidence:* §5.4, Table 2, "the stale memory... crosses L = 0.40 near episode 300... ends at 0.17."
- **Why worth adopting for an NPC memory service:** Our reflection mechanism (post-August, not built) is meant to prune `identity_components` and drive identity revision, currently sampling "episodes weighted by importance × recency, not recent-N" — an outcome-linked signal is a plausible third axis. Concretely, our `agents.reputation` delta already IS a per-turn outcome signal (the dialogue call emits a clamped delta after each turn); in principle, correlating which memory IDs were surfaced in a turn's retrieval/gate/reconstruction set with that turn's reputation delta sign/magnitude gives us a nearly-free MW-style estimator, since we already log memory IDs per read (an existing invariant: "Read endpoints always return memory IDs and scores alongside prose") and already have an outcome scalar (reputation delta) per turn.
- **Adoption cost/risk in our stack:** Meaningful design work, not a schema-level drop-in, and the paper's own failure-mode experiments are a real caution for us specifically: our retrieval is already **not** the "uniform random" regime the convergence proof assumes (assumption A3, conditional independence of retrieval from outcome) — our gate/decay/importance-weighted retrieval is exactly the kind of policy-coupled retrieval the paper's own Experiment 3 (retrieval-policy feedback) and task-difficulty-confound Experiment 2 warn about. The paper's honest finding that global MW can be *directionally wrong* (−0.33) under confounding that closely resembles our situation (memories retrieved preferentially in specific narrative/emotional contexts, à la their "hard task" stratification) means this would need real validation, not a naive port — likely as a reflection-time diagnostic signal rather than a real-time retrieval-scoring term.
- **Docs it would touch:** Would live in the not-yet-built reflection spec (`architecture.md` reflection section, sequenced-later ledger); not a current build target, and no schema change is implied beyond possibly a `memories`-adjacent counter pair if ever adopted.
- **Confidence:** Low-Medium for direct adoption (the paper itself flags this as association-not-causation and shows real, sometimes severe, failure modes under exactly the kind of policy-coupled retrieval we already have); Medium-High as a "worth knowing this exists" research-track note for when reflection is eventually specced.

### THESIS-TENSION flags (conflicts with an invariant — surface, don't force)
- **Invariant challenged:** "Importance vs relevance are independent axes. Importance scored once at write time (stored raw, normalized at read)."
- **What the paper does that conflicts:** MW is explicitly designed to revise a memory's assessed quality *after* write time, based on downstream outcomes rather than write-time scoring — the paper's own framing positions this as a direct critique of exactly our kind of static write-time importance score: "Write-time importance scores are static... a simple, general per-memory estimator of post-retrieval outcome association... has not been proposed" (Abstract).
- **Honest read:** This is a genuine positioning tension worth naming honestly rather than dismissing. Our invariant is deliberate — importance anchors to a per-NPC integrator "diagnosticity goal" precisely so it stays stable and predictable rather than drifting with noisy outcome feedback, and MW's own failure-mode experiments (task-difficulty confound flipping the signal's sign; co-retrieval confound requiring ≥30% retrieval diversity to even separate two memories with a 17× true utility gap) demonstrate that an outcome-linked signal is fragile exactly under the kind of narratively-coupled, non-uniform retrieval our gate and decay math intentionally create. This is not evidence our static-importance design is wrong — it's evidence that *adding* an outcome-linked axis on top would need real care (likely as an advisory reflection-time signal, never replacing write-time importance) rather than being adopted as a fix.

### Quotable lines / citations for positioning (optional)
- "When an agent stores a memory, it makes an implicit prediction: this piece of knowledge will be useful in the future. That prediction may be correct at the time of storage but become wrong..." (§1) — a clean framing line for why write-time importance and post-hoc quality are genuinely different questions, useful if we ever write up the importance-vs-relevance-vs-(future)-outcome distinction.
- "p+(m) is an associational quantity, not a causal one" (§4.2) — worth citing as the honest caveat if this idea is ever pursued, to avoid overclaiming what such a signal would mean for us.

### Verdict
P3 note-only: a well-reasoned mechanism with real teeth in its own failure-mode analysis, but not adoption-ready for our stack given how far our retrieval already is from the paper's convergence assumptions; worth flagging as a research-track idea for the eventual reflection spec (a cheap version is nearly free given our existing memory-ID + reputation-delta logging) rather than acting on now.
