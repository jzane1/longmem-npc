## Sleep-time Compute: Beyond Inference Scaling at Test-time

- **Authors / venue / year:** Kevin Lin, Charlie Snell, Yu Wang, Charles Packer, Sarah Wooders, Ion Stoica, Joseph E. Gonzalez — 2025 (Letta / UC Berkeley)
- **arXiv / DOI:** arXiv:2504.13171v1
- **Source:** folder
- **Overall relevance to longmem-npc:** Medium-High — maps closely onto the not-yet-built reflection endpoint and the "later/optional" dormant-agent overseer idea; largely *validates* (rather than beats) the already-built reconstruction pre-warm mechanism.
- **Core contribution (2-3 sentences):** "Sleep-time compute" precomputes useful re-representations of a context during idle time, before the user's query is known, so that test-time inference can be much cheaper for the same accuracy. On modified GSM-Symbolic and AIME reasoning benchmarks, sleep-time compute cuts the test-time compute needed for equal accuracy by ~5x, and scaling sleep-time compute further raises accuracy by up to 13-18%; when multiple related queries share one precomputed context, per-query cost drops a further 2.5x. Effectiveness correlates strongly with how *predictable* the eventual query is from the context — pure novelty is not amenable to precomputation.

### Mechanisms relevant to us
- **Offline/idle-time precomputation of a re-represented context, served cheaply at test time** (§3, "sleep-time compute... S(c) → c′... at test-time we can use a much smaller test-time budget b ≪ B") — this is essentially the same core idea already implemented as our reconstruction engine's pre-warm-at-dialogue-init + decay-band cache (baseline: "pre-warm at dialogue init; mid-scene miss → block + expose a 'reconstructing' signal"). The paper is external validation of pre-warming as a legitimate, quantified latency-hiding technique, not a new mechanism for us to add.
- **Amortizing one precomputed representation across many future queries** (§5.3, 2.5x cost reduction at 10 queries/context) — partially already present via our `(memory_id × identity_version+band)` reconstruction cache and the batched-call-per-scene design, but that amortizes *repeat reads of the same memory*, not compute shared across an NPC's *many future dialogue turns/sessions* the way an offline reflection pass would.
- **Query-predictability as the gating factor for when precompute helps** (§5.4) — a real caution: precomputing character state changes only pays off when the eventual player interaction is structurally anticipatable, which is exactly why our reconstruction pre-warm targets the *known* current memory set (bounded, anticipatable) rather than trying to guess arbitrary future dialogue content.
- **Scaling sleep-time compute itself (parallel generations) for further accuracy gains** (§5.2, up to +13-18%) — a possible not-yet-evidenced extension to reconstruction's one-shot batched retelling call, but the paper's gains are measured on math-reasoning correctness, which has no clean analogue to "retelling quality," so this is speculative for us (kept low confidence below).

### STRICTLY-BETTER candidates (beats a mechanism we already have)
*(none)* — reconstruction's pre-warm + cache already implements the paper's core idea (compute ahead of the query, serve cheap at test time); the paper offers no evidence that its specific technique (parallel-sampled re-representations at sleep-time, or reasoning-effort scaling) beats our particular batched-retelling-call design, since the domains (symbolic math accuracy vs. character-telling drift) aren't comparable enough to claim a win.

### NOT-YET-BUILT candidates (a capability we simply don't have)
- **Capability:** A scheduled/idle-time (sleep-time) compute pass for the reflection endpoint, and more generally for the "dormant-agent memory-injection overseer" idea — both currently require an integrator to manually pull a trigger rather than running during genuine idle time.
- **What the paper does:** Runs inference on the available context *before* the query arrives and anticipates likely future needs, cutting test-time compute ~5x for equal accuracy and amortizing that cost across future related queries by up to 2.5x — *evidence:* Abstract, "by anticipating what queries users might ask and pre-computing useful quantities, we can significantly reduce the compute requirements at test-time"; §5.3, "we can decrease the average cost per query by 2.5×."
- **Why worth adopting for an NPC memory service:** reflection today is "an endpoint (no scheduler); store exposes reflection pressure as a readable gauge; integrator pulls the trigger" (baseline) — a manual, synchronous-when-triggered design. Sleep-time compute reframes this as a background job that runs when the NPC (or the game) is genuinely idle (between scenes/sessions), pre-revising identity/pruning components ahead of the next dialogue call rather than paying that cost inline. The dormant-agent overseer idea (sequenced-later ledger, "wake trigger = context match") is essentially this exact pattern applied to an NPC that is asleep between player visits.
- **Adoption cost/risk in our stack:** Real but bounded — needs an actual scheduler/background-worker surface (none exists today; reflection is currently "endpoint, no scheduler" by explicit design choice), and the query-predictability caveat (§5.4) means it only pays off for reflection/identity work that's anticipatable from current state, not for genuinely novel future dialogue. This is squarely a reflection-mechanism design question, which is already flagged as post-August and unbuilt — nothing here forces urgency, but it is a concretely evidenced pattern to reach for when that spec session happens.
- **Docs it would touch:** `docs/architecture.md` §12 (reflection & parameter compiler, designed/not-built), the sequenced-later ledger entry for the dormant-agent overseer.
- **Confidence:** High that the mapping is real and the pattern is well-evidenced; Medium on transfer since the paper's benchmarks are symbolic-reasoning accuracy, not identity-revision quality.

### Quotable lines / citations for positioning (optional)
- "sleep-time compute, which allows models to 'think' offline about contexts before queries are presented: by anticipating what queries users might ask and pre-computing useful quantities, we can significantly reduce the compute requirements at test-time" (Abstract)
- "sleep-time compute can reduce the amount of test-time compute needed to achieve the same accuracy by ∼5×" (Abstract)
- "By amortizing sleep-time compute across related queries about the same context... we can decrease the average cost per query by 2.5×" (Abstract)
- "sleep-time compute is most effective in settings where the query is more easily predictable from the context" (§5.4) — the caveat worth carrying into any reflection-scheduling design.
- "sleep-time compute builds on the idea of pre-fetching in traditional operating systems, in the context of LLMs à la Packer et al. (2023)" (§2) — notable: Packer et al. 2023 is MemGPT, by the same Letta authors; worth knowing MemGPT's own team frames sleep-time compute as the OS-prefetching analogue to MemGPT's paging, a useful side note if MemGPT ever comes up as a destructive-compression counter-example in the README.
