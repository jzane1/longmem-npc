# When Does Belief-Based Agent Memory Help? Reliability-Conditional Updating and Provenance-Capped Poisoning Defense (Nous)

- **Authors / venue / year:** Pranav Singh (IIT Ropar) — July 2026, arXiv preprint (self-described as "a mechanistic study rather than a leaderboard entry").
- **arXiv / DOI:** arXiv:2606.22030v2 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22030
- **Source:** discovered
- **Overall relevance to longmem-npc:** Medium-High — proposes a concrete, honestly-scoped mechanism for *when* probabilistic conflict-resolution beats simple overwrite, and a provenance-based trust model that maps cleanly onto our existing `typology`/`provenance` columns; most directly relevant to our still-unbuilt diegetic-correction/dissonance path, not the (already-built) authorial-correction replace model.
- **Core contribution (2-3 sentences):** Nous stores each entity-attribute pair as a categorical probability distribution updated by a closed-form Bayesian posterior weighted by per-observation "reliability," with entropy decay as forgetting and a stored "delta" (not the raw record) as the audit artifact. The paper's central, unusually honest finding is that this machinery is *inert* on the standard LoCoMo benchmark (ties naive last-write-wins) because ordinary conversational QA rarely presents contradictory, differently-trustworthy evidence — but once a reliability signal is supplied (extracted from epistemic language markers), belief-updating recovers a large, mechanism-attributable advantage (100 vs 67 vs 46) on a purpose-built contradiction/staleness benchmark. A follow-on section shows content-inferred reliability is adversarially gameable, and that capping trust by *source provenance* (not content confidence) is what actually defeats a volumetric memory-poisoning flood (0% vs 100% attack success).

### Mechanisms relevant to us
- **Reliability-conditional Bayesian belief update per entity-attribute "dimension"** (§3.1–3.4): posterior update `p_post(v) ∝ L(obs|v)·p_prior(v)` where the likelihood is governed by a per-observation reliability `r`; entropy of the distribution rises via exponential mixing toward uniform when unobserved (`p_t = γ^t p_0 + (1-γ^t) U`), giving principled "growing uncertainty" distinct from deletion.
- **The reliability-signal-dependency finding** (§7–8): a controlled ablation shows the Bayesian machinery only earns its keep when a genuine per-observation trust/reliability signal exists; without one it degenerates to a "soft recency-follower" indistinguishable from last-write-wins.
- **Trust composition rule: `r = min(provenance, content)`** (§9) — content-derived confidence can only *lower* trust within a channel's provenance ceiling, never raise it; this single design choice is what resists the volumetric-poisoning and "laundering through a trusted intermediary" attacks (with taint-tracking as the (unimplemented) requirement for the latter).
- **Set-valued attribute handling via delta-log aggregation** (§3.6) — because a unimodal posterior can't represent "all places visited," Nous keeps a full delta log alongside the current-belief mode and re-aggregates at query time for list-type attributes, worth noting as a caution against naive Bayesian-belief adoption for any of our multi-valued fields (e.g., `entities`).

### NOT-YET-BUILT candidates (a capability we simply don't have)
- **Capability:** Reliability/provenance-weighted arbitration between conflicting *observed* facts, feeding the not-yet-built diegetic-correction / dissonance mechanism (baseline: "Dissonance (designed, mechanism post-August)... Dissonance threshold = importance × evidence typology × per-NPC rigidity"; "Diegetic correction... Mechanism is post-August (not built)").
- **What the paper does:** Derives a per-claim reliability score from epistemic-confidence language ("maybe"/"I think" → low; "confirmed"/"definitely" → high) and threads it into the Bayesian posterior update, then separately caps that signal by the observation's *source provenance* so an untrusted channel cannot buy its way to high trust via confident phrasing — *evidence:* §8, "statements written to be confident receive mean reliability 0.95, hedged ones 0.23"; §9, "provenance-capped updating... holds volumetric memory-poisoning at 0% attack success where naive baselines reach 100%."
- **Why worth adopting for an NPC memory service:** Our schema already has the two ingredients this needs — `typology` (`observed|told|inferred|reflected` + `typology_confidence` + `typology_source`) is exactly a content/source-provenance distinction, and the dissonance formula already multiplies by "evidence typology." Nous's contribution is the specific, honestly-scoped claim that arbitration mechanisms like this are worthless on ordinary dialogue and only pay off when genuinely conflicting, differently-reliable evidence is possible — which is precisely the diegetic-correction/dissonance scenario (an NPC hears contradictory claims from the player vs. its own ground-truth record) rather than the authorial-correction scenario (operator fixes data, no ambiguity). This is a positioning point as much as a mechanism: it argues our dissonance design should treat "player says X, but the store says Y" as the reliability-conflict regime, and should resist letting confidently-worded player assertions override the immutable record without an explicit provenance/trust gate (`typology_source = declared` from a player vs. `inferred` from NLP, or an eventual "player-provenance" tier) — echoing OWASP's provenance-tracking recommendation the paper cites.
- **Adoption cost/risk in our stack:** Low for the conceptual reuse (our schema is already shaped for it); non-trivial for the actual Bayesian-posterior machinery, which is a genuinely different storage model (a distribution per entity-attribute) than our bi-temporal supersede chains, and the paper's own ablation shows the naive version underdelivers without a real reliability extractor — so the honest cost is "design the reliability signal for our domain" (e.g., calibrating from NPC-witnessed vs. player-claimed vs. rumor-relayed provenance), not "port the code."
- **Docs it would touch:** `docs/architecture.md` §7 (dissonance mechanism, still designed-not-built) and its eventual own spec session, whenever the diegetic-correction target is pulled forward; not a migration or current-build touch.
- **Confidence:** Medium — the paper is explicit that its headline reliability result is validated on a synthetic contradiction benchmark, not real dialogue ("a real-dialogue contradiction slice is the natural next step"), and the LoCoMo comparison is "indicative not controlled" (different backbones/retrieval stacks). Treat the mechanism as a well-argued design pointer, not a benchmarked drop-in.

### THESIS-TENSION flags (conflicts with an invariant — surface, don't force)
- **Invariant challenged:** Non-destructive bi-temporal storage / "the ground-truth record underneath never lies."
- **What the paper does that conflicts:** Nous's Bayesian posterior is explicitly *unimodal and lossy by default* — "Bayesian posteriors are unimodal: after many updates, mass concentrates on one value, discarding historical alternatives" (§3.6) — it only preserves the full history via a supplementary delta-log workaround for list-valued attributes, not as the core storage model.
- **Honest read:** This is a genuine architectural mismatch, not just a different domain. Our invariant requires every prior belief state to remain queryable forever (superseded rows stay live via `invalid_at`, never deleted/collapsed); Nous's core data structure is designed to *forget* discarded alternatives from the live posterior by construction, treating the delta log as an afterthought rather than the ground truth. If we ever adopted Bayesian belief-tracking for conflict arbitration, it would have to sit as a read-time *scoring* layer over our existing immutable fact-version chain (informing which head to trust/surface), never replace the chain itself as the system of record — the paper's own delta-log aggregation escape hatch is functionally closer to what our chain already does than to the posterior itself.

### Quotable lines / citations for positioning (optional)
- "The brain does not store a verbatim transcript of past conversations; it maintains a generative model of its environment and updates it when observations diverge from predictions." (§1) — useful contrast/positioning line: our thesis keeps the verbatim transcript (the immutable record) and puts the "generative model" behavior in the *telling* layer (reconstruction), which is arguably a cleaner separation of the same intuition Nous is reaching for by conflating storage and belief.
- "the belief update was never the difficult part; provenance propagation is." (§9) — good citation if we ever write up the dissonance mechanism's trust model.

### Verdict
P3 note-only for now: no adoption action is warranted before the diegetic-correction/dissonance target is even scoped, but it's a strong forward-reference for that future spec session — specifically, treat "reliability-conditional arbitration only helps when genuine source-reliability variance exists" and "trust must be provenance-capped, not content-inferred" as design inputs to surface then, not now.
